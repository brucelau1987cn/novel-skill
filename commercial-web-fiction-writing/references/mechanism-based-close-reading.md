# Mechanism-Based Close Reading for Commercial Web Fiction

Use this reference when comparing or diagnosing contiguous chapter ranges in经营文、种田文、无限流、规则怪谈、惊悚副本 or other mechanism-heavy commercial fiction.

## Scope and Evidence

- Read the exact requested range and group the report by the user's boundaries, while retaining chapter-level notes internally.
- Follow canonical `下一章` links and verify displayed headings. Stop at the first page whose article body is empty or access-controlled; report the last readable chapter and do not infer the missing prose from its title.
- Keep extracted notes transformative and compact. Record mechanisms and state changes, not chapter text.

## Unified Chapter Ledger

For every chapter, capture only the fields that change:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| Stage goal | What concrete result must be achieved now? |
| Competence action | What learned skill, labor habit, social skill, or prior experience becomes an observable action? |
| Resource delta | What is gained, spent, hidden, damaged, transferred, upgraded, or made renewable? |
| Rule delta | What rule is introduced, tested, narrowed, overridden by identity, or made costly? |
| Information gap | What do protagonist, reader, allies, antagonists, and system each know? |
| Space/map delta | Does the move create a new constraint, institution, supply source, or threat ecology? |
| Payoff | What promised pleasure, safety, status, clue, relationship shift, or tool is actually delivered? |
| Ending cut | What exact action, consequence, rule change, or question remains live? |

A profession is plot-functional only when it follows:
`recognize -> choose -> act -> alter resources/permissions -> create the next consequence`.
Do not count résumé exposition or praise from bystanders as competence payoff.

## Resource Accounting for经营文

Track public and concealed ledgers separately:

- **Public ledger:** food, cash, tools, land, labor, transport, reputation, favors, official permission, family capacity.
- **Concealed ledger:** space inventory, modern supplies, secret contacts, hidden identity, unreported knowledge.
- **Conversion:** show how one resource becomes another, such as cooking skill -> service value -> travel permission -> survival capacity.
- **Cost and provenance:** every concealed withdrawal needs an explanation, concealment cost, depletion risk, or exposure risk.
- **Reuse test:** a meaningful reward should affect a later decision. A tool or currency number that never returns is decorative rather than operational.
- **Renewability:** distinguish consumables from recurring production capacity. Seeds, tools, recipes, labor training, and institutional access usually matter more than one meal.

Flag "temporary inventory rescue" when a needed medicine, tool, duplicate garment, or supply appears only after the crisis begins and had no prior inventory boundary or cost.

## Rule Lifecycle for无限流 and规则怪谈

Track each rule through:
`statement -> interpretation -> test -> exploitation -> cost/counter-rule`.

Classify rule power precisely:

- spatial: applies only in a zone;
- temporal: applies during a shift, countdown, weather, or phase;
- identity-based: tourist, employee, monster, contractor, prisoner, etc.;
- procedural: knock, scan, pay, report, carry a tool;
- hierarchical: one administrator or department can amend another's instruction;
- economic: access consumes currency, electricity, data, wages, health, or time.

Identity conversion is a plot event, not a loophole by itself. Record who grants the identity, its duties, visible proof, privileges, revocation conditions, and price.

Flag "rule rescue" when an authority can issue a perfectly tailored update after danger appears without an established permission boundary, audit trail, competing authority, or cost.

## Information-Gap Matrix

Maintain a compact matrix rather than merely saying "the protagonist has information advantage":

| Party | Reliable information | False belief | Blind spot | Ability to act |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Then ask whether the information gap creates:

- anticipation: readers know danger before the character;
- puzzle pleasure: readers can infer alongside the character;
- dramatic irony: another character misreads competent action;
- coercive leverage: the protagonist controls others' choices;
- unfair omniscience: decisive information arrives only when needed and could not be inferred.

## Tone Mixing

For comedy + hardship or workplace comedy + horror, inspect sequence and residue:

1. What establishes real cost before the joke?
2. Does humor emerge from character worldview and coping behavior, or interrupt from outside the scene?
3. After the joke, does injury, death, fear, or moral consequence retain weight?
4. Does the same comic translation repeatedly neutralize every threat?

A stable mixed tone changes the interpretation of danger without deleting danger. Reserve some deaths, irreversible choices, and world revelations for unbroken emotional aftermath.

## Stage Goals, Map Changes, and Unit Continuity

A map change is meaningful only if it changes at least two of:

- governing rules or institution;
- resource economy;
- available roles/identities;
- threat type;
- social composition;
- time horizon;
- route back to the long-term goal.

At every unit boundary, audit continuity across:
`character consequence + carried resource + unresolved promise + antagonist pressure + world mystery`.

If only the protagonist's generic ability carries over, the story risks episodic fragmentation. Carry at least two concrete consequences from the prior unit into the next.

## Payoff and Chapter-End Audit

Separate payoff from hook:

- **Payoff:** the chapter delivers a promised result or meaningful partial result.
- **Hook:** the ending creates a live next action, consequence, reversal, clue, threat, or changed rule.

Prefer endings on irreversible state change or a newly actionable fact. Diagnose repeated cuts by mechanism, such as repeated assault interruption, repeated identity suppression, repeated "monster approaches," or repeated public humiliation. Repetition is justified only when it escalates cost, changes knowledge, reverses power, or pays off an older promise.

## Strong-Payoff Nine-Axis Audit

Use this audit when the user asks for 强爽点、开篇留存、换嫁/重生反杀、系统或玄术绑定, or a chapter-grounded comparison. A "mechanism" need not be a literal system: rebirth memory, reciprocal illness, legal marriage, social rank, a spatial artifact, professional knowledge, or a secret can all bind characters and generate repeatable plot.

For every requested block, inspect all nine axes and attach chapter numbers to each material claim:

1. **Mechanism binding:** identify what forces continued interaction, what each party supplies, why either side cannot simply leave, and whether the bond is bodily, economic, legal, emotional, informational, or secret-sharing.
2. **Task escalation:** state the opening survival problem, the next-stage objective, and the level change: private -> household -> institution -> state/world. Escalation must change authority, cost, opposition, or time horizon rather than merely add another rude opponent.
3. **Immediate reward:** record the latency between action and payoff. Distinguish emotional release, recovered property, legal status, witness/ally, money/tool, new permission, relationship commitment, and information. Prefer rewards that become usable in the next conflict.
4. **Antagonist use:** assign each antagonist a function: hatred loading, public humiliation setup, evidence delivery, competence demonstration, institutional pressure, map transition, relationship catalyst, or long-line threat. Ask whether the antagonist acts from coherent self-interest and whether the protagonist can convert that hostility into leverage.
5. **Lead relationship:** track the transaction first, then trust, protection, shared secrets, public identity, physical proximity, jealousy/intimacy, and irreversible commitment. Separate what the pair says the relationship is from what their costly actions prove.
6. **Power limitation:** inventory activation conditions, bodily cost, cooldown, capacity, provenance, secrecy/exposure risk, legal risk, counters, and failure cases. Repeated fainting is one cost pattern, not a complete limitation system; note when it becomes a reusable transition button.
7. **Information gap:** specify who knows the mechanism, future event, identity, evidence, or hidden motive. Show how the gap produces anticipation, irony, coercion, or a reversal; flag convenient omniscience and opponents who ignore available evidence.
8. **Hook:** identify the live next question or action created inside the chapter, not just at its final sentence. Track hook rotation across threat, reveal, decision, relationship turn, evidence, rule change, and promised confrontation.
9. **Chapter ending:** name the exact cut mechanism: interrupted action, identity declaration, incoming authority, result withheld, danger arrival, new clue, irreversible choice, or completed payoff plus successor threat. Diagnose repeated cuts even when the wording differs.

### Evidence Discipline

- Use a compact internal row per chapter: `chapter/title -> mechanism/task delta -> payoff -> relationship delta -> cost -> antagonist function -> information gap -> ending cut`.
- In the final answer, cite the smallest chapter set that proves the claim, such as `第4-6章`, and explain the causal chain. Do not scatter chapter numbers after broad unsupported summaries.
- Separate observed event from interpretation: `第6章玉佩碎裂` is evidence; `先做最小可验证奇迹` is the transferable inference.
- When a block ends at the public-access boundary, say exactly which chapters were read and do not infer the next chapter from its title.
- For two works, finish each work's full segmented analysis before comparing. Compare equivalent functions, not merely similarly named tropes.

### Transfer, Fatigue, and Logic Risk

Express transferable craft as causal templates, for example:

- `small verifiable proof -> authority witness -> social resource -> larger-stage access`;
- `antagonist's self-interest -> predictable choice -> protagonist's information advantage -> antagonist unknowingly grants the desired status`;
- `temporary transactional bond -> costly rescue -> shared secret -> public commitment`;
- `resource recovered -> immediately reused -> next conflict becomes possible`.

Diagnose repetition by the reader experience it produces. Count recurring `insult -> retaliation -> shocked crowd`, accusation of impurity/status, public group ridicule, convenient rescue, fainting after power use, and rival-love-interest confrontations. A recurrence earns its place only if it changes cost, knowledge, authority, relationship, or outcome.

Run a separate logic-risk pass for:

- legal and institutional consequences of assault, drugging, trespass, arson, coercion, marriage, military privilege, or access to controlled people;
- powers whose capacity, activation, storage, time ratio, healing, or concealment has no meaningful bound;
- political or bureaucratic procedures compressed beyond the story's own realism contract;
- death/revival rules introduced only when needed;
- antagonists made implausibly vile or foolish solely to authorize retaliation;
- a sharp drop in conflict scale after a major revenge payoff.

## Final Comparative Output

For each requested range:

1. Summarize the stage engine in one sentence.
2. Cover the requested axes with chapter-numbered causal evidence rather than plot recap.
3. Reconcile resource, rule, relationship, and information ledgers.
4. State payoff latency, antagonist function, hook rotation, and chapter-end pattern.
5. Name one structural gain, one accumulating fatigue pattern, and one logic risk.

End with:

- transferable methods expressed as causal templates;
- genre-specific differences that should not be flattened;
- a separate fatigue list and logic-risk list;
- the exact public chapter boundary and any claims left unverified.
