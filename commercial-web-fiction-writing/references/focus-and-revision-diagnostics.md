# Focus and Revision Diagnostics

## Core Three Questions

Answer each in one sentence:

1. What urgent goal must drive the protagonist through the story?
2. What deeper motive and root emotion make that goal necessary?
3. What unique selling point makes this version worth reading?

If these cannot be stated clearly, plot selection will remain unstable.

## Opening Checklist

- Can readers immediately identify the protagonist?
- Are predicament, need, and objective clear?
- Is the core selling point demonstrated rather than merely announced?
- Are central conflict, long-term goal, and current task/crisis established?
- Is there a strong hook consistent with title and blurb?

Delete or relocate unrelated material. Compress background into active scenes.

## Necessity Test

Every paragraph, sentence, and detail should do at least one job:

- move the protagonist closer to or farther from the goal;
- reveal character, motive, contradiction, or change;
- reveal indispensable setting or world information;
- create the scene's intended atmosphere or emotion;
- satisfy or intensify the target reader's core expectation.

If it does none, delete it. If it repeats a useful function, compress it.

## Structure and Viewpoint

The final goal and current-stage task govern scene selection. A subplot, new character, or setting element must advance the main plot, shape the protagonist, strengthen conflict, or serve the reader contract.

Anchor early narration to the protagonist. Supporting viewpoints require information the protagonist cannot know or an essential emotional effect, and should remain brief and purposeful.

## Show Through Consequential Detail

Use choices under pressure, action, dialogue, gesture, physical reaction, and selective interiority. Do not replace emotional development with labels such as “she was devastated.” Environment should create current mood, affect action, or hint at evidence/danger.

## Climax Design

Define the payoff first. Build focused pressure, crisis, humiliation, desire, or misunderstanding around the core conflict. Avoid unrelated material near the emotional peak. At release, fully deliver the intended pleasure, pain, revelation, or relationship shift.

## Genre Contracts

Examples:

- cultivation/fantasy: growth, power, breakthrough, combat hierarchy, reversal;
- period rebirth: revenge or repaired regret, information advantage, mobility, antagonist defeat, career and relationship resolution;
- romantic angst: relationship movement, attraction, emotional tug, interaction tension, and credible turning points.

Research current category leaders to refine rather than mechanically copy these contracts.

## Editorial Feedback Translator

### “Prose needs improvement / weak detail / insufficient impact”
Look for direct emotional labels, surface-only description, redundancy, low-information dialogue, and viewpoint fragmentation. Replace with concise, consequential action and sensory or psychological detail at key moments.

### “Entry point lacks attraction / weak immersion / too much background”
Start at conflict, suspense, danger, or a consequential change. Move explanation into action. Reduce early cast size.

### “Slow pacing / no hook / confused viewpoint / weak protagonist presence”
Identify the main conflict, remove unrelated material, restore the protagonist as the decision-maker, anchor viewpoint, and add a long hook plus chapter-level expectation.

### “Flat or unrealistic character / weak agency”
Rebuild `identity -> desire -> motive -> choice -> consequence`. Give the protagonist active decisions and costs, not merely reactions to plot events.

### “Main plot unclear / weak expectation”
Rebuild `goal -> obstacle -> action`. Check whether the promised category payoff is actually pursued. If a core hook closes, seed the successor before payoff.

### “Tone or style inconsistent”
Check whether later content violates the title, blurb, category, emotional mode, or primary experience that acquired the reader.

## Finding Format

For each issue, state:

1. Location or scene.
2. Observable defect.
3. Reader consequence.
4. Operation: delete, compress, move, strengthen, or rebuild.
5. Target effect after revision.
